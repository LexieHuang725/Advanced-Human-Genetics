# Lesson01 - Case Study
> A 68 y/o assigned female at birth (AFAB), Mrs. Jones, is referred to
genetic counseling by her breast surgeon after having a biopsy of her
left breast. The pathology results identified ductal carcinoma in situ
(DCIS), a non-invasive form of breast cancer. Her doctor is very
concerned because this is the second cancer diagnosis for Mrs. Jones.
She was diagnosed with and treated for papillary serous ovarian cancer
with a total abdominal hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-
oophorectomy, followed by chemotherapy, at the age of 48. In 2003,
due to her personal history of ovarian cancer and family history of breast
cancer, her oncologist ordered BRCA1/2 testing that was negative.

1. List the “red flags” for hereditary cancer present in Mrs. Jones’ personal medical history.
   * first dx before age 50
   * multiple relatives having cancer history
   * rare cancer history (ovarian cancer)
   * two cancers diagnosed

2. Generate a list of questions to ask Mrs.Jones during the appointment, especially regarding her family history (i.e. what additional information will help you to formulate a probable diagnosis?)
   * ask if she wants multiple gene panels other than BRCA
   * ask about occupations, tobacco use, alcohol use
   * ask about timing of manalpause
   * ask for any estrogen medication dosage
   * ask for insurance or resources she demands/have/prefer
   * what other genetic testing she had other than BRCA or what genetic testing her relatives have had done
   * get whole family size, limited structure or not, if anyone is adopted
   * other genetic prone condition

3. Review the family history form that Mrs. Jones completed in the waiting room. Then, use this information to complete the pedigree provided.

4. List the “red flags” for hereditary cancer present in the family history
   * cancer history across multipla generations
   * almost all females in her family had cancer history

syndromes make up your differential
diagnosis?
* mother has ovarian cancer, multiple cancer histories across multiple generations, high risk of hereditary
* Hereditary and Famaliar
* rare cancer, young onset
* suggest other genetic panels for
  * lynch (HNPCC) - colon, GI cancer
  * HBOC
  * Li-Fraumeni Syndrome (TP53) - breast, ovarian cancer
  * hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (HDGC) (CDH1 mutation) - stomach and lobular breast cancer risks
  * Multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN1 or RET mutation) - thyroid
  * Just single site of BRCA1/2, ask about what specifically the test sequenced

   * benign tumor-like growths (hamartomas), increased cancer risk, and neurodevelopmental disorders.
   * PHTS syndrome
   * BRRS disorder

```
True Negative - Definition: A test showing no harmful variant when a specific mutation is already known within the family.
Uninformative Negative (Noninformative) - Definition: A test showing no mutations when no previous genetic variant has been identified in the family.
```
