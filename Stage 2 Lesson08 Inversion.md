# Lesson 8 - inversion
## Definition
* deletion(del): a loss of any part of a chromosome
* duplication(dup): an additional copy of a segment of a chromosome
* inversion(inv): a segment of a chromosome is reversed end to end
* Insertion(ins): a segment from one chromosome is intercalated into the same or
another chromosome

### Types of inversions:
* Pericentric inv: break in the short (p)
arm and long (q) arm; involves the
centromere
* Paracentric inv: breaks in either the
short arm or long arm; no centromere
involvement

### Autosomal Pericentric inversion
* The inversion heterozygote is phenotypically normal
* may produce unbalanced gametes due to recombination within the inverted segment during meiosis
* inverted homolog and normal homolog don't perfectly line up -. A LOOP FORMS TO ALIGN ALL SEGMENTS
* Crossing over within loop causes 4 possible gametes:
  1. Normal homolog: aa’
  2. Inverted homolog: bb’
  3. Recombinant homolog: cc’dup(p)
  4. Recombinant homolog: dd’dup(q)
* aa’ and bb’: balanced
* cc’: duplication of part of the short arm and deletion of part of the
long arm
* dd’: duplication of part of the long arm and deletion of part of the
short arm
* Only inversions with small distal segments will have viable
abnormal offspring; abnormal offspring from inversions with large
distal segments are not viable

### X chr pericentric inversion
* X inversions form the same way, but with different implications
  - breakpoints in the X critical region may influence the phenotype of the **female**
    - gonadal dysfunction: primary amenorrhea or premature menopause
    - stature genes(eg.SHOX) located on Xp; ovarian genes on Xq
  - imbalance in the 46, X, rec(X) female may be mitigated by skewed activation of the abnormal
  - X with variant Turner syndrome
    - Del(Xq)/dup(Xp): normal or tall stature and ovarian dysgenesis
    - Del (Xp)/dup(Xq): short stature and intact ovarian function
    - <img width="343" height="286" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/33babf94-c705-4657-97e0-bd85d064c80f" />
* X inversions can be transmitted by **both males and females**
* Males inv(X) hemizygotes: no effect on phenotype or reproduction
  - No recombination possible at meiosis
  - All daughters will be heterozygotes and all sons receive the normal Y

### Common inversions
* **Inv(Y)(p11q13)** is not uncommon in general population
  - no phenotypic or reproductive effect
  - considered a **normal variant**
* Autosomal common inversions: **inv(2)(p11q13)** and inversions of 1, 9, 16, and Y heterochromatin
  - no phenotypical effects and are considered normal variants

### Risk of having an affected offspring
* Ascertainment via recombinant child: 5-15%
  - actual risk depends on specific inversion
* no family history of recombinant form: overall 1%
  - individual risk depends on the specific inversion
* analyze the family history, check in the literature, assess the degree of imbalance, etc.
* prenatal diagnosis should be offered to the following:
  - any heterozygote whose family had a recombinant child (“heterozygote” means an inversion carrier: one chromosome has the inversion, while its matching homolog has the normal arrangement.)
  - any heterozygote for an inversion (or large inv) on this table

### Paracentric inversion
* do not involve the centromere
* **theoretically cannot produce unbalanced offspring**
* if a rec is formed, the gametes would be acentric or dicentric(nonviable)
   = 0.1-0.5% risk of abnormal offspring (we never say never in genetics)
• Exception if seen in literature
• Paracentric inversions are difficult to detect on classical cytogenetic
analyses; good quality, high-res banding is required
X and Y:
* Familial **paracentric inv(X) has no phenotypic consequence**, but **breakpoints in the critical region may
compromise fertility**
* Few cases of inv(Y) have been reported: Liou et al. (1997) reported a normal grandfather and father
with a **46,X,inv(Y)(q11q21)**
  - **Grandson had ambiguous genitalia with internal mullerian structures**
