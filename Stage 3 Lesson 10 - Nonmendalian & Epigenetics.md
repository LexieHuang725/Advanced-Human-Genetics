# Lesson 10 - Nonmendelian & Epigenetics
## Nonmendelian inheritance
* Not all gens from both parents equally distribute to offspring, and not equally expressed
* **Types**
  - mitochondrial inheritance
  - mosaicism
  - UPD
  - genomic imprinting
## Epigenetics
* study of changes in gene function that can be inherited, do not entail in DNA sequence change
* normal epigenetic functions: control embryonic and somatic cell function, X-inactivation, genomic imprinting
* **Types**
  - Genomic imprinting and UPD are part of epigenetics
  - other biochemical factors:
    - methylation of CpG dinucleotides
    - acetylation
    - methylation of histones
    - other changes in chromatin
* epigenetic changes are very importance in disease biology

## Genomic imprinting
* differential gene expression depending whether it is inherited from mother or father -> causing same genetic material but different phenotype
* affects gene expression but no sequence change
* a reversible form of gene inactivation, not a mutation
  - reversible process: **imprinting conversion**
* regions have imprinting effect: rich in CpG islands

## Clinical examples of imprinting
### Triploidy (69,XXX, 69,XXY, 69,XYY)
* fetus carries twice the normal chromosome contribution from one parent
* **Prevelance**
  - 2% of all conception
  - usually result in early spontaneous abortion(unusual to survive to term)
* **Clinical Features**
  - **2 paternal + 1 maternal haploid**
    - large cystic placenta + partial molar changes
    - large head
    - intrauterine growth delay
    - syndactyly(并指)
  - **1 paternal + 2 maternal haploid**
    - small, underdeveloped placenta
    - underdeveloped fetus
    - placenta failure
* paternal genetic information is important for placenta development and maintenance
* **Etiology**
  - 90% paternal derived
    - 66% double fertilization
    - 24% due to fertilization with a diploid sperm
  - 10% maternal: fertilization of a diploid egg

## Chromosome deletion syndromes because of imprinting
### Prader-Willi syndrome(PWS)
* **Clinical features**
  - hyperphagia(constant hunger)
  - hypogonadism
  - ID
  - small hands and feet
* **Etiology**
  - 70% 15q11-13 deletion(**paternal origin**)
    - 50% a microdeletion can be detected cytogenetically
    - 20% a deletion of variable size can be detected by FISH
  - 30% maternal UPD(so no paternal allele)

### Angelman syndrome(AS)
* **Clinical features**
  - happy disposition(always happy, frequent laughter)
  - ID
  - bizarre, repetitive and symmetric ataxic movements
  - seizures
  - typical face
* **Etiology**
  - 70% 15q11-13(**maternal origin**)
    - 50% can be detected cytogenetically
    - 20% detected by FISH
  - 5% have paternal UPD
  - small number of patients have mutation in maternal copy of gene(E6-AP ubiquitinprotein ligase),
   which is normally expressed only from maternal allele in CNS
  - 10-20% unknown

## UPD
* mostly occurs when a trisomy cell line early in embryogenesis converts to a 
 disomy cell line -> 1/3 tims a UPD forms
* **Clinical consequences:**
  - imprinting effect
  - AR disorder
* **Types**
  - heterodisomy: homologous chromosomes are unidentical
  - isodisomy: the two homologous chromosomes are identical
