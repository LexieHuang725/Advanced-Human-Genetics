# Lesson 5 Aneuploid Syndromes
## Down Syndrome, 47, XX/XY, +21
* 1/600 newborns
* Diagnosis usually suspected after birth
* Diagnosis made by chromosome analysis
### Clinical features
* Mild-moderate intellectual disability
* Short stature
* Hypotonia
* Dysmorphology:
  ✓ Small brachycephalic head
  ✓ Flat nasal bridge
  ✓ Epicanthal folds
  ✓ Upward-slanting palpebral fissures
  ✓ Small mouth with protruding tongue
  ✓ Small ears
  ✓ Excessive skin at nape of neck
  ✓ Short 5th finger with clinodactyly
  ✓ Single transverse palmar crease
* Hearing loss
* Obstructive sleep apnea
* Otitis media
* Eye disease including cataracts and severe
refractive errors
* Congenital heart defects
* Neurologic dysfunction, gastrointestinal
atresia, hip dislocation, thyroid disease,
diabetes, premature aging, dementia and
leukemia
### Medical Management
* No treatment
* Directed at addressing individual medical concerns
* Home environment, early intervention, education, vocational training can impact functioning level
* Anticipatory guidance given to family

### Mosaic down syndrome
* Two populations of cells, some with 46
chromosomes, some with 47 (extra 21)
* Nondisjunction occurs during one cell
division after fertilization
* Mosaic trisomy 21 individuals may have
milder phenotype
  * Can give parents a false sense of optimism

### Recurrence risks
* Nondisjunction trisomy 21: 1% for any autosomal
trisomy or mother’s maternal age-related risk at her
next pregnancy (whichever is greater)
* Translocation: determine if inherited from parent
  – 25% - 50% of translocation cases are inherited
  – Depends on sex of parent
    - 10-15% if mother is 14/21 carrier
    - Less than 5% if father is 14/21 carrier
    - 100% if isochromosome 21/21(an abnormal chromosome where the short arm (p) is lost and replaced by a mirror-image copy of the long arm (q))
* Prenatal diagnosis available

## Trisomy 18 (Edwards Syndrome)
* Pre- and post-natal growth retardation
* Polyhydramnios
* Single umbilical artery
* Hypertonia
* Microcephaly
* Rocker bottom feet
* Clenched hands, overlapping
fingers
* Severe intellectual disability
* Heart, kidney and genitourinary
malformations
* Diaphragmatic hernia
* Limited capacity for survival
  – Apnea; feeding, respiratory, cardiac, CNS problems
  – 30% die within the first month, 50% by two months, 10%
* survive to first year as severely intellectually disabled
  – Most die early in embryonic or fetal life
* Etiology: nondisjunction
  – Risk increases with advancing maternal age
  – Rarely, translocations
* Recurrence risk:
  – **1% for any chromosomal aneuploidy**

  – Risk increases with advancing maternal age

* Recurrence risk:

## X-inactivation
Lyonization - begins **early in ambryonic development**
- All descendant cells have same X chromosomes active or inactive
- Dosage compensation
- 
### X-inactivation center (XIC)
**Located at Xq13**
XIST(X inactive specific transport) gene:
  * *Cis*-acting gene transcribed from inactive X
  * function as an RNA molecule, coating chromatin and prevents DNA from being transcribed
  * inactive state is locked in by GpG island methylation

## Pseudoautosomal regions (PAR)
* PAR1 and PAR2 are not inactivated (males have PAR on both their X and Y chromosomes, females do not need to silence them. Thus, PAR1 and PAR2 genes naturally escape inactivation.)
  - 29 genes
* SHOX gene is thought to be associated with human diseases
* About 20% of other non-PAR genes escape inactivation as well

## Turner syndrome
<img width="400" height="453" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cf7bb757-587f-4b3c-b67d-af9193f78f00" />

- 99% of conceptions result in embryonic death
– 1/2000 to 1/5000 female live births
– Can have mosaicism or partial deletion of X, usually less severe

### Clinical features
* Decreased birth weight and slow growth

* Short stature

* Lymphedema of hands and feet
* Webbed neck
* Low posterior hairline
* Cubitus valgus
* Strabismus
* Micrognathia
* High arched palate
* “Shield chest”
* Gonadal dysgenesis (a congenital condition where the ovaries or testes fail to develop properly in embryonic development)
  > - Ovariandegeneration during fetal development,
typically no functional ovarian tissue
  > 
  > - **Estrogen replacement therapy is essential during
adolescence**
  > - If mosaic (45,X/46,XY), **exploratory laparotomy** (a major open abdominal surgery used to diagnose and treat unexplained pain, severe bleeding, infections, trauma, or organ damage when non-invasive tests like imaging are inconclusive) indicated because of increased risk for gonadoblastoma
* Thyroid disease
* Hypertension
* Hearing loss
* Renal abnormalities
* Cardiovascular abnormalities
* Glucose intolerance
* Hyperlipidemia
* Intellectual disability uncommon
  – Learning disabilities can occur, especially in visual-
  spatial organization, math and memory
  – Behavioral abnormalities (e.g. hyperactivity)
  – If intellectual disability, search for a structural
  rearrangement of the X chromosome
* Etiology
  – Typically **paternal X or Y is missing**
  – **Not associated with maternal age; sporadic(random)**

## Klinefelter syndrome - 47, XXY
<img width="400" height="385" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e81188ef-22a9-46fe-8373-c0340588587c" />

* 15% are mosaic 46,XY/47,XXY; better
prognosis for testicular function
* Severity increases with **number of extra X
chromosomes**
* 1 in 1000 male births
* Frequency increased in azoopermic infertile
males (1/10) and males with significant
learning difficulties (1/100)
* Diagnosis generally made during adult life
during infertility investigation
  – **Most common cause of hypogonadism and
infertility in males**
* Learning disabilities
  – 15-20% have IQs below 80
* Behavior problems: immaturity,
insecurity, shyness, unrealistic
boastful, assertive activity
* Tall, slender body habitus
* Testes and penis are small,
testosterone values are low
  – Poorly developed secondary sexual
characteristics
  – Gynecomastia occurs in ~40%
  – Obesity as adults without testosterone replacement

## 47, XXX
1 in 1000 female births
• Maternal meiotic nondisjunction w/maternal
age effect
• Phenotypically normal
• 75% are fertile, offspring usually normal
• 15-25% have mild learning difficulties
– Severity increases with number of extra X
chromosomes

## 47, XYY
• 1 in 1000 male births
• Paternal meiotic nondisjunction
– No apparent paternal age effect
• Phenotypically normal
– Taller than average male
• IQ score 10-15 points less than sibs
– Behavior problems may occur

### CGC - when do we order a karyotype:
* for patients with obvious chromosomal syndrome
* family history of chromosomal rearrangement

If a translocated chromosome is not clear to see in karyotype, we can order **FISH** to help

### When do we order Microarray:
* postnatal evaluation:
  * multiple congenital anormalies and no known syndrome
  * developmental delay, intellectual disability
  * ASD
* Prenatal:
  * one or more major structural anormalies in ultrasound in pregnancy
