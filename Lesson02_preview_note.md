### Coefficient of Relationship (r)
measure the proportion of genes that two related people have in common

calculation can be helpful in providing recurrence risks for AR trait in a family with consanguinity

$${r=(1/2)^n}$$

3<sup>rd</sup> degree relatives: 1/8

2<sup>nd</sup> degree relatives: 1/4

1<sup>st</sup> degree relatives: 1/2

n = degree of relatives

| Relationship           |         Calculation |   \(r\) |
| ---------------------- | ------------------: | ------: |
| Parent–child           |         \((1/2)^1\) | \(1/2\) |
| Full siblings          | \((1/2)^2+(1/2)^2\) | \(1/2\) |
| Grandparent–grandchild |         \((1/2)^2\) | \(1/4\) |
| First cousins          | \((1/2)^4+(1/2)^4\) | \(1/8\) |

### Coefficient of Inbreeding (F)
The probability that a homozygote has received both alleles at a locus from the same ancestral source
$${F=r/2}$$

3<sup>rd</sup> degree relatives: 1/16

2<sup>nd</sup> degree relatives: 1/8

1<sup>st</sup> degree relatives: 1/4

## Cystic Fibrosis
* disorder of epithelial ion transport
* cause by mutation in CFTR gene -> result in abnormal fluid and electrolyte transport across epithelial apical membranes
* diagnosis initially made clinically, based on pancreatic and pulmonary findings and elevated level of sweat chloride

### Major Clinical Features
* onset - neonatal to adulthood
* meconium ileus
* progressive pulmonary disease
* growth failure
* exocrine pancreatic insufficiency
* obstructive azoospermia
* elevated sweat chloride concentration

### Management
* no curative treatment
* manage symptoms
  - improvements have increased average longevity to 30-40 years
  - have CFTR modulators targeted to variants
* clearance of pulmonary symptoms
* control of pulmonary infection
* pancreatic enzyme replacement
* adequate nutrition
* prevention of intestinal obstructions
* lung transplant

### Genetic Testing
* newborn screening in 50 US states and most Canadian provinces
* diagnoss in symptomatic individuals
* carrier testing
* prenatal diagnosis
* preimplantation genetic diagnosis

## Hemoglobinopathies
* Sickle cell disease: abnormalities in *structure* of hemoglobin
* Thalassemias : abnormality in amount of hemoglobin, resulting from imbalance in relative amount of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ chains

## Hemoglobin
* oxygen carrier in vertebrate red blood cells
* tetramer - consisting of four subunits
  - two $\alpha$-globin chains
  - two $\beta$-globin chains
* each subunit = polypeptide chain, globin, heme
  - heme is an iron-containing pigment that combines with oxygen to give the molecule its oxygen-transporting abillity

## Beta globin gene cluster - chromosome 11
<img width="811" height="371" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/323ca553-e7de-4ac8-ae60-bde5783e695c" />
* different genes in beta gene clusters are expressed at different times in development, called "globin switching"
  * expression is due to the locus control region(LCR)

* production of beta can start at 6-8 weeks gestation, but is minor
  * transition from Hb F to Hb A mostly occurs after birth, by 3 months of age. Therefore if they have issue at beta chain, we can start to see sign when they are 6 months old.

* Normal adult:
  * Hb A: 96-98%
  * Hb A<sub>2<\sub>: <2-3%
  * Hb F: 1-2%

Wide range of mutation types leading to reduced levels of $\beta$-globin gene expression from affected chromosome
  * often point mutation (vs. large deletions more common in alpha globin cluster)
  * $\beta$<sup>0<\sup>: complete absence of $\beta$-globin
  * $\beta$<sup>+<\sup>: reduced but detectable $\beta$-globin

Heterozygotes (aka thal minor/trait) usually unaffected
  * or mild anemia, often confused with iron deficiency

Homozygous (or compound heterozygous) state:
  * thalassemia major or cooley's anemia
  * thalassemia intermedia (eg.2 $\beta$<sup>+<\sup> muts)

### Screening for carriers of Hemoglobinnopathies
* any pregnant person is recommended to test mean corpuscular volume(MCV): measure the size of red blood cells
  - 80-100 um<sup>3</sup> is normal, MCV < 80 warrants further testing
  - differential includes iron deficiancy, beta thal trait, alpha thal trait
* serum ferritin studies
* Hb electrophoresis: separate the molecules based on size and charge
  - people with alpha thal trait have a normal Hb electrophoresis
    - b/c alpha is present in all 3 types of Hb in equal proportions
  - people with beta thal trait have more Hb A<sub>2</sub>
    - b/c delta tries to make up for the beta deficiency

> | HbF        | \(\alpha_2\gamma_2\) |                     <1% |

Alpha-thalassemia trait: 

Alpha-thalassemia reduces production of alpha chains. But all three major hemoglobins require alpha chains:

$${HbA_2=\alpha_2\beta\_2} $$
$$ {HbA_2=\alpha_2\delta_2} $$
$$ {HbF=\alpha_2\gamma_2} $$

Therefore, alpha-chain deficiency affects all these hemoglobins rather than selectively increasing one type. Their relative proportions may remain approximately normal, so standard adult hemoglobin electrophoresis is often normal in alpha-thalassemia trait.

This does not mean hemoglobin production is completely normal. The patient can still have:
* microcytosis: low MCV;
* small, pale red blood cells;
* mild anemia.
* There are exceptions: newborns may have detectable Hb Bart’s ($\gamma<sub>4</sub>), and more severe alpha-thalassemia can produce HbH ($\beta<sub>4</sub>).

Beta-thalassemia trait:
Beta-thalassemia reduces production of beta chains. Therefore, less HbA can be formed:

$$ {\alpha_2+\beta_2\longrightarrow HbA} $$

The available alpha chains can instead pair with delta chains:

$$ {\alpha_2+\delta_2\longrightarrow HbA_2} $$

Consequently, the proportion of HbA₂ increases, commonly to greater than 3.5%, which is a key finding in beta-thalassemia trait.
