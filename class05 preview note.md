## Meiosis and Mitosis
### Mitosis:
* Occurs during **cell division of somatic cells**
* A diploid cell generates an identical diploid cells
* Normally, no recombination takes place

### Meiosis
* Occurs during **gamete formation**
* A diploid progenitor cell generates four haploid gametes
• Homologous chromosomes from parent are allocated randomly to daughter
cells
• **Recombination is frequent**

## Definitions
* Chromosome
  * Rod shaped structures of condensed nucleic material (DNA)
  * Come in pairs, contain genetic material
  * Unique characteristics including different sizes
  * Humans have 46, or 23 pairs – 22 pairs of autosomes (non-sex); 1 pair of sex chromosome
* Karyotype - organized picture of chromosomes
* Homologous chromosome - a pair inherited from different parents (1-22 pairs and XX)

* Centromere - Constriction on chromosome where sister chromatids are held together

* Kenetochore - protein structure on the centromere to which
the spindle fibers are attach

* Sister chromatids - two identical strands of a replicated chromosome, joined by the centromere

* Kinetochore microtubules - responsible for pulling the sister chromatids
of each chromosome apart

* Polar microtubules - overlap in midline and responsible for pushing the poles of the spindle apparatus apart
  (mechanically split the cell)
* Astral microtubules - radiate in all directions and aid in spindle
orientation immediately before mitosis

* Centrosome - organelle that is the primary microtubular
organizing center that splits and forms the
spindle apparatus

* Centrioles - located in the centrosomes and produces the
microtubules

* Spindle apparatus - network of filaments overall responsible for
separating the chromosomes during cell division

### M stages
#### 1. Prophase
  * Chromosomes condense (DNA progressive selective packaging along protein scaffolding)
  * As condensation happens, high/less condensed regions form light/dark bands.
  * Each chromosome contains two strands of DNA (sister chromatids) connected tgt at centromere
  * Centrosomes divide and migrate towards opposite poles of the cell
<img width="686" height="608" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e959090c-1dc9-4318-b1de-7331cf2af125" />

#### 2. Prometaphase
  * Nuclear envelope disrupts and breaks into membrane vesicle
  * Spindle microtubules enter nuclear region
  * Kinetochores mature and attach to kinetochore microtubules
<img width="530" height="645" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/44e82c3b-ad2b-4b6a-af2c-229e701d13f5" />

#### 3. Metaphase
  * Nuclear membrane disappears, chromosomes become maximally condensed, easily visible on light microscope
  * Kinetochore microtubules align chromosomes at equatorial plate midway in cell
  * Polar microtubules begin to associate to prepare for anaphase
<img width="660" height="497" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e0164779-55b2-459e-b975-fbe4fd44b98f" />

#### 4. Anaphase
  * Centromeres abruptly separate
  * Each sister chromatid is pulled to its opposite pole by kinetochore microtubules
  * Two phases of movement are described:
    * kinetochore microtubules shorten and draw chromosomes towards spindle poles
    * polar microtubules elongate forcing two poles of spindle further apart
  * Arrival of chromosome at the spindle poles = **end of anaphase**

#### 5. Telophase
  * Spindle apparatus begins to dissociate
  * Polar microtubules elongate, and further separate the two poles
  * A nuclear envelope begins to form about aggregated chromosomes at each pole
  * Chromosomes begin to decondense and nucleoli reappear
  * Mitosis is at end and cell enters sytokinesis

#### 6. Cytokinesis
  * Occur by cleavage, which began during anaphase
  * A cleavage furrow develops around mid-section of the cell
  * Furrow deepens until it meets remnants of spindle apparatus and form small bridge-like midbody
  * Midbody breaks down and separate the daughter cells

## Meiosis
* Process of gamete formation
* onsists of two successive cell divisions
* Meiosis I has four stages analogous to mitosis: prophase,
metaphase, anaphase, and telophase
* Prophase in meiosis I is much more complex and may
span (in the oocyte) for 40 years or more
* At the end of prophase I, chiasmata are observed – posi-
tions where chromosomes touch and crossing over has occurred
between chromatids of homologous chromosomes
* Chromosome breakage and rejoining occurs resulting in
recombinant chromosomes
* A pair of homologues held together by chiasmata is called
a **bivalent**
* Crossing-over between paternal and maternal chromatids
creates endless possibilities of recombination and genetic varia-
bility
<img width="276" height="709" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cd4157de-81c9-4887-af49-08b2d66129ee" />

* Crossing over also occurs between the X and Y chromo-
somes at pseudoautosomal (matching sequences on the X and Y
chromosome).
* In rare instances, genetic recombination occurs outside the
**pseudoautosomal region (PAR)** resulting in XX males and XY females.
* The **SRY gene** (sex-determining region) lies near the
pseudoautosomal boundary on the Y chromosome and is respon-
sible for male sex determination.

### Mendal's Principles
* **Mendel’s Law of Segregation** – gametes con-
tain only one homologous chromosome (and
therefore one gene for each trait)
* **Mendel’s Law of Independent Assortment** –
chromosomes (and genes) are inherited inde-
pendently from other chromosomes **(possibilities = 2<sup>n</sup>)**

### Gametes - Spermatogenesis & Oogenesis
<img width="638" height="459" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/33be7014-c2a6-4bda-8fa1-d477a8abe962" />

* Four spermatids are formed from every primary spermatocyte; the process is
relatively short (60 to 70 days)
* Prophase of the primary oocyte starts during fetal life, remaining in “suspended”
phase until the oocyte matures and is ovulated
* Meiosis I is completed at the time of ovulation; one daughter cell receives 23
chromosomes, most of the cytoplasm and becomes a secondary oocyte; the
other 23 chromosomes are extruded as the first polar body
* The second meiotic division proceeds almost immediately, but is only completed if fertilized in the fallopian tube; the second polar body is extruded
* Thus, only one mature ovum with a haploid chromosome complement is
formed (In oogenesis, the cell does not divide its cytoplasm equally. It wants to make one large egg with lots of cytoplasm, organelles, nutrients, and machinery for early development. So when the chromosomes separate, almost all the cytoplasm stays in one daughter cell, while the other daughter cell gets very little cytoplasm. That tiny cell is the polar body.)
* The intricate and prolonged meiotic process in the oocyte, with a presumably
high likelihood for error, is the **most likely explanation for advanced maternal age to have increased risk of chromosomally abnormal conceptions**. It is well known that the origin of the **additional
chromosome 21 in most cases of Down syndrome is maternal**.
* The majority of cases of Down syndrome are caused by **nondisjunction** - failure of two chromosomes in a pair to disjoin during anaphase of cell divi-
sion. Both homologous chromosomes from one parent (segregate) into one daughter cell during either meiosis I or II.

#### Genetic Consequences
**List the 4 genetic consequences of meiosis**
1. Reduction of chromosome number
- Diploid 2n → haploid n
- Humans: 46 → 23
- This lets fertilization restore the diploid number instead of doubling it every generation.

2. Segregation of alleles
- Homologous chromosomes separate in Meiosis I.
- This is the cellular basis of Mendel’s Law of Segregation.
- Each gamete gets only one allele for each gene.

3. Independent assortment
- Different homologous chromosome pairs orient independently at Metaphase I.
- Therefore maternal and paternal chromosomes are randomly distributed into gametes.
- In humans, independent assortment alone gives 2²³ possible chromosome combinations per gamete.

4. Genetic recombination / crossing over
- During Prophase I, homologous chromosomes exchange DNA between non-sister chromatids.
- This creates recombinant chromosomes with new combinations of alleles.
