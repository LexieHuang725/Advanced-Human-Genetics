#### Autosomal Reciprocal Translocation
* 1/500 individuals are translocation carriers
* typically 2-way translocation involving two chromosomes
* translocation carrier at risk of having child with multiple congenital anomalies
* **unbalanced** if a segment of **one chromosome is duplicated** and **other segment is deleted**

<img width="671" height="336" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/aad0bbe5-cd36-400e-b4b5-2227523e287c" />
* Simple reciprocal translocation: two-way exchange between two crhomosomes
* Rearranged chromosome is called a derivative(der) chromosome
* The der chromosome is defined by its centromere
* - exchanged segment: translocated segment
  - uninvolved segment: centric segment
* Balanced translocation have **no** loss/gain of genetic materials
* Single-segment exchange: when one of the translocated segment is small and comprises only telomeric region of a chromosome [t(1;4)]
* Double-segment exchange: when both translocated segments are large [t(5;10)]

### Meiotic Behavior
* At meiosis, the four chromosomes with segments in common come together as a "quadrivalent"
* In meiosis Prophase I, pachytene is when the crossingover takes place. The four chromosomes form a cross-shaped to match homologous segments
* Segregation - distribution of the homologs to the poles

> **What is a lop-sided quadrivalent?**
> - When this structure is described as "lop-sided," it means that one of the involved chromosomes is much smaller in genetic content than the others. This size asymmetry creates physical instability during cell division.

<img width="847" height="534" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ec13f7af-1658-480d-8adf-db7dafcbd2b6" />

| Rule | What to look for | Likely outcome | Memory cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Alternate segregation | Common; usually gives a normal or balanced result | **Alternate = alright** |
| **2** | Compare unbalanced gametes | The *least imbalanced* one is most likely to produce a viable abnormal conceptus | **Least imbalance survives best** |
| **3** | Translocated segments are small | **Adjacent-1** is most likely to produce a viable abnormal offspring | **Small translocated → A1** |
| **4** | Centric segments are small | **Adjacent-2** is most likely to produce a viable abnormal outcome | **Small centric → A2** |
| **5** | One *whole chromosome* in the quadrivalent is small | **3:1 segregation** is most likely | **One whole chromosome small → 3:1** |
| **6** | Rule 3 or 4 **plus** Rule 5 features | Both an adjacent pattern and **3:1** may produce viable offspring | **Two cues → two possible viable patterns** |
| **7** | Both translocated and centric segments are large | No segregation pattern is expected to produce a viable unbalanced offspring | **Both large → none viable** |
| **8** | Subtelomeric translocation | May form two separate bivalents, which segregate independently | **Near the ends → pairs may split apart** |

### GC part
* A liveborn aneuploid offspring demonstrates
viability for that abnormal combination to
recur (if one of parents carries a balanced translocation and has already had a liveborn child with a particular unbalanced chromosome combination, that combination is known to be compatible with survival to birth.)
* Ascertainment by miscarriage or infertility is
more difficult to predict
  – Risk must be determined by the specific
translocation and the size of the chromosome
segments involved

#### Magnitude of risk
**Four factors**
- The mode of ascertainment
– The predicted type of segregation leading to
potential viable gametes
– The sex of the transmitting parent
– The assessed imbalance of the potentially
viable gamete
* Risks range from 0% to 30% and depend on the
actual cytogenetic imbalance

| Factor | What it means | Why it changes the estimate |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Mode of ascertainment** | **How the translocation was first discovered**—for example, after a child was born with an unbalanced translocation, after repeated miscarriages, or incidentally. | A family found because of an affected child was selected *because* an adverse outcome occurred. Their risk estimate may differ from that for a carrier found incidentally, so estimates account for how families entered the data.) |
| **2. Predicted segregation pattern** | How the four paired chromosomes are expected to separate during meiosis: **alternate, adjacent-1, adjacent-2, or 3:1**. | Different patterns make different gametes. Alternate segregation usually gives a normal or balanced chromosome set; other patterns can give gametes with extra or missing chromosome segments. The pattern helps predict which unbalanced gametes are possible. |
| **3. Sex of the transmitting parent** | Whether the egg or sperm came from the translocation carrier. | Egg and sperm formation—and the selection of gametes or embryos afterward—can differ. Sex can affect empirical risk, but the direction and size of the effect depend on the specific translocation; don’t assume one sex always has higher risk.|
| **4. Imbalance in the potentially viable gamete** | The specific chromosome material that would be duplicated or deleted if that gamete were fertilized. | A small or less harmful imbalance may allow an embryo to survive, while a larger imbalance may lead to miscarriage or may not support development. The chromosome regions and genes involved matter as well as the amount of material. |

### Robertsonian Translocation
<img width="666" height="384" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b4335454-13d2-4f55-a1f2-6bf286474b84" />
* involve **acrocentric D and G**
group chromosomes (centromere very close to one end)
* The translocation chromosome
has **two fused long arms and no
short arm**
* Frequency: 1 in 1,000
* Balanced carriers have a
karyotype of *45,XX,der(14;21)(p10;q10)*

<img width="507" height="379" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ef19e57c-60b8-499f-9acf-ddcd99448c3c" />

* Most robs are **balanced** and
involve **two different
(heterologous) chromosomes**
* Homologous robs are rare
  – usually de novo
* Heterologous robs can be
transmitted through
generations with no
phenotypic effects
* **Most common: rob(13q;21q) and the
rob(14q;21q)**
  – *Most commonly involved in
translocation Down syndrome*
<img width="344" height="515" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/68863637-686b-4d99-9c19-e7c7598ae85b" />

* Three possible mechanisms for the
heterologous translocation:
  a. Centric Fusion
  b. Union following breakage in **one
short arm and one long arm**
    – Whole arm reciprocal translocation
    – Both produce a translocation chromosome
with one centromere
    – Centric fusion and whole arm translocations
are rare
  c. Union following breakage in **both
short arms**
    – produce dicentric chromosomes
    – **rob(13q;14q) and rob(14q;21q) are
usually dicentric**

### NOR (nucleolar organizer regions)
* The acrocentric chromosome contain the nuceolar organizer regions located in the stalks short arms
  - they contain the genes coding for rRNA
* When the rob forms, the NORs of the two fusion chromosomes are lost
* Individuals with robs have 8 vs. 10 NORs
  - Not all NORs are functioning
  - individuals need 4-7 active NORs for normal cellular function

### Heterologous Robertsonian Translocation
* At meiosis, the translocation chromosome and the two normal homologs synapse as a trivalent
* Following 2:1 segregation, six types of gametes are produced
  - Alternate segregation produces
normal and balanced gametes
– Adjacent produces two types of
disomic and nullisomic gametes
* 3:0 segregation is rare
<img width="300" height="200" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/bc021aa7-c91a-4414-bb2f-3984c2b084be" />

<img width="493" height="442" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/5aaae4a0-d760-43ea-aa4b-80e7b5150407" />

* Only unbalanced conceptuses with trisomy 13
or 21 survive
* Fetal trisomies 14, 15, and 22 miscarry
* Alternate segregation is favored in males
  – In male carriers translocation Down syndrome and
translocation trisomy 13 are rare
* Alternate segregation is also favored in female
carriers
  – Females have ~20% risk of abnormal offspring

#### Postzygotic Correction and Uniparental Disomy
* Translocation trisomy may be corrected
by mitotic loss of one of the
untranslocated homlogs
* Adjacent segregation produces a trisomy
15 conception
  – Loss of the other chromosome 15 corrects the
trisomy, resulting in UPD
  – UPD has no effect for chromosomes 13, 21, and 22
* UPD has imprinting effects for
chromosomes 14 and 15
* UPD from a parental rob is rare,
however, any fetus with a balanced rob
for chromosomes 14 or 15 must be tested

#### Homologous Robertsonian Translocation
* Homologous robs are comprised of
two of the same acrocentric
chromosomes
* Only two outcomes are possible at
meiosis 1:0 segregation:
  – The gamete is disomic
  – The gamete is nullisomic
  – No gametes are balanced
* Only translocation trisomy 13 and 21 are viable

* In **male carrier**, the risk for translocation DS is 1% at amnio
