* Threshold effect
  - threshold of normal vs. mutant proportion mtDNA within the cells of the tissue -> above threshold: clinical symptoms
  - more mutant mtDNA, more severe symptom
  - proportion of normal vs. mutant mitochondria can be different from cell to cell/tissue to tissue
  - #mutant mtDNA needed to cause cell dysfunction differ tissue to tissue
    - depend on vulnerability to oxidative metabolism impairment(different cells have different energy demands, metabolic flexibility, and mtDNA dynamics)
## segregation during divisions:
* during cell divisions, mutant mitochondrial propotion NOT distributed evenly between daughter cells
  - example of genetic drift at cellular level (gene variant (allele) frequencies in a population change across generation due to random chance)

* *heteroplasmy* and *threshold* explain why clinical symptoms within family can vary for mt disorders
## Clinical considerations
* clinical manifestations express in every organ system
* tissues and organs depend more on oxidative metabolism are more susceptible
* classification is hard due to overlapping clinical features
* **general clinical features**
  - **CNS symptoms:** seizure, myoclonus, ataxia, stroke-like episodes, dementia
  - **peripheral nervous system(PNV):** myopathy, neuropathy
  - **somatic:** endocrine(thyroid), cardiac(cardiomyopathy), deafness, renal disease, short stature
## Kearns-Sayre(KS) syndrome
* **Clinical features**
  - progressive external ophthalmoplegia(PEO), <20 yo onset
  - pigmentary retinopathy
  - one of below:
    - heart block, cerebellar syndrome, cerebrospinal fluid protein >100mg/dl
  - poor prognosis
  - most die in 30s/40s
* **Etiology**
  - majority of patients have sporadic mtDNA deletions
## MERRF(myoclonic epilepsy and ragged red fibers)
* **Clinical features**
  - myoclonus(involuntary muscle twitching)
  - generalized seizures
  - cerebella ataxia
  - ragged red fiber in muscle biopsy
* **Etiology**
  - majority have point mutation in tRNA<sup>lys</sup>
  - maternal inheritance
## MELAS(mitochondrial encephalopathy, lactic acidosis, and stroke-like episodes)
* **Clinical features**
  - stroke before 40 yo
  - encephalopathy featured by seizures and dementia
  - lactic acidosis(Lactate accumulation)
  - at least two of below:
    - normal early development, recurrent headaches, or recurrent vomitting
* **Etiology**
  - majority have A->G point mutation at position 3243 in tRNA<sup>leu</sup> gene
## NARP(neuropathy, ataxia, and retinitis pigmentosa)
* **Clinical Feature**
  - development delay
  - retinis pigmentosa
  - dementia
  - seizures
  - ataxia
  - proximal weakness
  - sensory neuropathy
* **Etiology**
  - point mutation in ATPase gene at bp 8993
  - maternlly inheritance
* When this mutation is very abundant, it produce a fatal infantile encephalopathy - aka Leigh syndrome)
* NARP and Leigh syndrome can exist in the same family -> clinical phenotype variability
* **Epigenetic factor**
  - alcohol and tobacco -> contribute to pathogenesis
## LHON(Leber's hereditary optic neuroretinopathy)
* **Clinical features**
  - acute unilateral central vision loss, then other eye vision loss within weeks
  - 18-30yo onset, more men affected(? X-linked modifying factor)
* **Etiology**
  - point mutation in various structural genes of mtDNA
  - multiple interacting mutations within the mitochondrial and/or nuclear genome are necessary for clinical symptoms
## Other important points
* point mutations maternally inherited
* deletion usually sporadic
* mutations in nuclear genes can cause mitochondrial disorders
