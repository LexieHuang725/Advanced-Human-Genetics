## AD definitions
* **Allele**: One of two or more alternative forms of a gene that arise by mutation
* **Locus**: Position on a chromosome of a gene or mutation
* **Autosome**: One of the chromosomes from 1-22
* **Heterozygous**: A wild-type allele paired with a mutant allele
* **Hmomozygous**: Both chromosomes have identical alleles
* **Hemizygous**: Have half of the alleles number
* **Dominant allele**: An allele whose phenotype is detectable in a single dose
* **Recrssive allele**: An allele whose phenotype is apperant only in the *homozygous/hemizygous* state

## Cancer Risk Factors
* Age
* Tobacco (*1/3 cancer cases*)
* Diet (high fat diet; alcohol consumption)
* Occupational exposures (asbestos(石棉))
* Hereditary factors (BRCA1/2 mutations)
* Radiation (long term UV exposure, atomic bomb)
* Virus exposure (Hep B, HPV)
* Chronic conditions (Crohn's, Celiac disease)

___
## Limitations
* Small family size
* Incomplete family history
* Inability to document diagnoses or incomplete relatives' genetc test result
* Preponderance of males (for breast cancer, men have much less risk, so BRCA mutation can be harder to track if family have little to no female relatives)
___
## Red flags
* Cancer diagnosed under 50 yo
* Multiple close relatives with same/related cancers
* ≥2 primary cancer diagnosis in same individual
* Certain rare cancer/tumor (ovary/pancreas)
* Other features associated with hereditary cancer syndromes

## Cancer Genes
* Tumor suppressor genes (TSGs)
* > Normal fxn: negatively regilate cell cycle progression, couple DNA damage recognition to cell-cycle control, promote apoptosis
  > Loss of function mutation can lead to tumor progression
* Oncogenes
* > Normal fxn: progress cell-cycle progression
  > Gain of function mutations
