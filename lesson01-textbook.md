## Naming of alleles
  For chromosomes, we have a short arm and long arm. For example, if we had 11p15, it means the region/band on chromosome 11 on the short arm.
  So the first number means the number of chromosome, p means short arm/q means long arm, and the second number means the specific band

## Genomic Imprinting
  Imprinted genes are expressed from only one parental allele. Only one copy out of two is expressed. DNA methylation marks determines which copy is expressed. Unmethylated allele expresses; unmethylated allele gets silenced. Each imprinted domain in chromosome regions is controlled by one or more indenpendent imprinted genes that regulate target expression in *cis* within the domain.

> As the image shows above, in *cis* regulation works on the same gene, affecting only the linked allele (eg.promoters,enhancers,operators), whereas in *trans* the regulation happens intermolecularly, and can affect both alleles equally (eg.TFs, repressors).

  An imprinting center (IC) is a apecialized DNA region that controls parent-specific gene expression and epigenetic marks across genes. It basically switch on/off to decide whether inherited gene is active/silenced.
  Epimutations: Epigenetic changes that impact ICs and cause gene transcriptional silencing.

  The first human disorders found to be caused by genomic imorinting were Prader-Willi sydrome and Angelman syndrome. In the 15q11-13 imprinted region, absence of paternal/maternal expressed genes lead to the diorders.
Imprinting disorders are features with molecular heterogeneity - several mechanisms cause disorder.
For example:
* Chromosome deletion
* Uniparental distomy (two copies of a single chromsome from one parent)
* imprinting defect (eg.epimutation)
* pthogenic sequence variants (eg.UBE3A in angelman syndrome)

## Beckwith-Wiedemann & Russell-Silver syndrome
BWS associates with in increased risk for embryonal tumors. 11p15 region has a imprinted gene cluster that is organized into two imprinted domains.
* IC1 domain in telomric region: contains IGF2 and H19 genes
  * IC1 is typically methylated on paternally derived chromosome -> H19 silencing
                                                                 -> IGF2 expression (cell growth&proliferaion promotion)
* IC2 domain in centromeric region: contains CDKN1C, KCNQ1, KCNQ10TI genes.
  * IC2 is typically methylated on maternally derived chromosome -> KCNQ10T1 silencing
                                                                 -> KCNQ1 and CDKN1C expression (negative regulator of cell proliferation)

  imprinting center 1 (IC1) in chromosome 11p15 region. Normally the paternal derived chromosome has methylated CpG site; whereas the maternal derived chromosome has unmethylated CpG site. This results in insulin-like growth factor 2 (IGF2) only from paternal allele.

  If paternal chromosome also has unmethylated site, IGF2 expression is suppressed and cause reduced growth, the baby develops Russell-Silver syndrome (RSS) - undergrowth.

  If maternal chromosome CpG site is also methylated, the activation of IGF2 promotes growth and causes Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (BWS) - overgrowth.
